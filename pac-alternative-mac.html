PAC for Mac? Alternatives for checking PDF/UA on a Mac

Updated October 2026.

If you work on a Mac and have been told to “check the PDF with PAC”, you will soon find out that there is no Mac version. Here are the options, with their strengths and limits.

A note on names: this app is called PDF Accessibility Checker, which is also what the acronym PAC stands for. The two are not related. PAC is developed by axes4 and the PDF/UA Foundation.

The tools

PAC

PAC (PDF Accessibility Checker) by axes4 and the PDF/UA Foundation is free and widely used as a reference for PDF/UA checks. It runs on Windows only. On a Mac you need a Windows virtual machine or a Windows PC.

veraPDF

veraPDF is open source and cross-platform; it is written in Java and runs on macOS. It validates the machine-checkable rules of PDF/UA-1 (and PDF/A). Its messages are technical – rule numbers and PDF object names – which suits developers and specialists more than authors.

Adobe Acrobat Pro

Acrobat Pro, available on Mac by subscription, has an accessibility checker and a full set of remediation tools to edit tags, reading order and alternative text. It is the most complete option for fixing documents.

PDF Accessibility Checker (this app)

A native Mac app that works offline. It explains problems in plain language and tells you how to fix them in the source program (Word, Pages, InDesign). It adds a screen reader view, guided manual checks and some automatic fixes (language, title, XMP repair, removal of the author and document metadata) in the Pro version.

What this app checks, and how well

PDF Accessibility Checker implements a subset of the automatic PDF/UA rules. We compared 19 of its checks with veraPDF: tags, PDF/UA identifier, title, language, untagged content, role map, figure alternative text, headings, tables, lists, font embedding, Unicode mapping, annotations, links, form field names, tab order, notes, encryption and artifact nesting. It also has checks that veraPDF does not have, such as declared language versus the real language of the text, and invalid XMP metadata.

How we measured it

We froze the app’s rules, then ran the app and veraPDF 1.30.3 on 250 public PDFs downloaded afterwards and never used before (Italian, US and European public bodies, companies; tagged and untagged). On the 248 files veraPDF could read, the two tools agreed on 99.8% of file-and-check pairs. All 19 checks agreed on 164 of the 172 tagged files (95.3%) and on 75 of the 76 untagged files. The differences were then analysed:

  • eight were false alarms of the app (for example metadata containing invisible control characters, which the app treats as invalid XML while veraPDF tolerates them, and some control codes in fonts);
  • two were problems the app missed: one, pages missing from a damaged structure index, has since been fixed; the other is a single page number in a table of contents, linked in an unusual way.

Being honest about the limits

PAC and Acrobat check more conditions than this app. For example, they examine how table header cells are associated with data cells (PDF/UA section 7.5), which this app does not fully verify automatically; it asks you to review table headers manually instead. If you need the broadest automatic coverage, use veraPDF or PAC alongside it.

And none of these tools – this app included – certifies legal conformance. Automatic checks find many problems, not all of them; a human review of alternative text, reading order and contrast is always needed.

How PDF Accessibility Checker helps

For Mac users who are not accessibility specialists, the app fills the gap between technical validators and full remediation suites: it tells you what is wrong in words you can act on, shows you what a screen reader will read, guides you through the manual checks and fixes the simplest problems for you.
